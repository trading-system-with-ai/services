# Risk Engine Spec Compliance — `prompts/risk_engine.md` §1–§74

*Merged compliance audit. Head of Market Risk, 2026-08-19. Seven independent auditors, one row per spec section, every claim traced to `file:line`.*

> **Verdict.** The platform has built a genuinely comprehensive statistical risk layer and preserved every pre-existing hard limit intact, but essentially none of it vetoes a trade today — it computes, logs and displays, and Tier 0 alone still decides.
>
> **结论。** 平台确实构建了一套完整的统计风险层，并完好保留了原有的全部硬性限额，但目前这些统计模型几乎都不能否决交易——它们只做计算、记录和展示，真正做决定的仍然只有 Tier 0 硬限额。

The `PROD` count rising 15 → 16 reflects metrics being instrumented, not a gate being promoted; the gates those metrics observe remain SHADOW.

**Does the current risk control cover `risk_engine.md`?**

Substantively yes on architecture and honesty; materially no on decision authority. Every hard limit that existed before the programme survives byte-identical — `assess()` (`libs/trading_core/risk/engine.py:436-694`) still runs its nine-gate ladder, and I independently confirmed that neither production call site (`apps/gateway/routers/orders.py:1748`, `apps/gateway/routers/income.py:333`) passes `extra_caps`, with an AST-level test (`tests/test_risk_adversarial.py:1664-1727`) that fires even on an accidental empty tuple. The programme *closed* a hard-limit hole (income opens previously bypassed the risk gate and wrote no `RISK_DECISION`) rather than widening one. On top of that sits a real statistical layer: a returns abstraction with enforced conventions, a model registry with health/mode vocabulary, Historical and Gaussian VaR/ES at 95 and 99, Euler ES contributions that sum exactly to ES, full option revaluation under scenarios, GARCH(1,1) with a genuine diagnostic battery, correlation regime detection, and a walk-forward Kupiec/Christoffersen validation harness. Explainability exceeds the spec: no bare risk score exists anywhere, and every number carries its model, version, confidence, horizon, sample size and health.

The gap is that the spec's decision hierarchy (§72) is shipped only down to its third tier. Account permission → data quality → hard limits bind; statistical, stress, concentration and model-health compute in shadow and bind nothing. That is *the state §70 explicitly recommends*, and the promotion seam is one reviewable line — but it means §27's "the Stress Test must retain veto authority" is not satisfied by shipped behaviour, and §74's "real decision value" is zero to date by construction. Worse, the evidence that promotion depends on was not accruing as designed: **zero trading days of shadow decisions have elapsed** (`risk-engine-final-report.md:11`), and seven of the nine §65 observability metrics were never instrumented, so there was no alertable time series recording how often a shadow gate *would have* bound. **UPDATE 2026-08-19 — the evidence pipeline is now fixed** (this audit's own recommendation #2): all nine §65 metrics are instrumented, and §59's missing model-risk budget effect now exists in shadow, so the window finally records a hypothetical quantity that model risk can actually move. The clock on the 20 trading days starts from a pipeline that measures what it claims to.

Two model-risk limitations were load-bearing and honestly disclosed. **The first is now closed (2026-08-19, compliance batch 2):** options no longer enter VaR/ES/contributions `DELTA_LINEAR`. A position carrying its chain contract's strike, right, tenor, IV and mark is priced by FULL REVALUATION — `pnl_t = qty·mult·[BS(S·(1+r_t)) − BS(S)]` — in the book P&L, the Euler contributions and the pre-trade candidate alike, so long-GAMMA tails are now measured rather than approximated (a long-call book's served ES-95 moves 541.90 → 480.18, the convexity direction). **What remains is narrower and now labelled on the wire:** the estimator is `CONST_IV` and holds `T` at `T0`, so long-VEGA and theta are still outside it; a spread is one book row carrying its long leg, so its modelled convexity is an upper bound; and an option whose chain gave no IV falls back to `DELTA_LINEAR` and says so per position key in `data_quality.pnl_method_by_key`. The second limitation is unchanged: tail dependence is not measured at all, with the correlation-convergence stress scenario as the standing proxy.

The deferrals are disciplined rather than evasive — each one is scored against the §62 six questions with concrete data numbers, carries a falsifiable re-visit trigger, and in the EVT/copula case is protected by adversarial tests that fail if anyone half-wires the model in. But **eight items were rated IMPLEMENT in the Phase A audit, never built, and never carried into the final report's Deferred Models table**, so they carried no rationale and no trigger anywhere. Those were the genuine compliance findings, listed in §3 below. The most consequential was §59: Model Risk State was computed, persisted and displayed but had no budget effect *even in shadow*, which silently removed a model-risk input from the very evidence window promotion is supposed to depend on.

**UPDATE 2026-08-19.** All eight Tier A items are now closed: seven were built (§11, §18, §34, §36, §37, §59, §65) and one (§30) was recorded as a documented deferral with a trigger, which is the remediation this audit itself offered as the alternative. Two further defects found while closing them are fixed and recorded in their rows: §45's `correlation_state` was a **declared field the builder never populated**, so the typed snapshot silently disagreed with the API for the whole of Phase C; and the §18 documentation defect (three docs disagreeing about Spearman) is closed by correcting all three. **The decision-authority verdict above is unchanged** — of everything added, only the observability metrics are PROD. Every new model computes, logs and displays, and Tier 0 alone still decides.

**UPDATE 2026-08-19 (compliance batch 3).** The remaining Tier C display, nomenclature and structural gaps are closed: a machine-readable §5 tier taxonomy now travels with every served and persisted metric row; incremental VaR (§8) and net vega (§46) are first-class pre-trade fields; ES contributions exist at 99 % with the audit's own noise objection shipped AS the row's health (§10); a live GARCH fit's ω/α/β and Ljung-Box diagnostics are persisted (§13); the stress endpoint accepts per-ticker shocks (§26); dispersion finally spans the stress family (§40); `stress` is a typed snapshot field (§45); `is_stale` has a consumer (§55); the DEVLOG §69 fields are backfilled; and the UI half landed with the four missing dashboard cards (§48), the per-position §52 columns, the §50 provenance modals and the §23 inline framing. **The decision-authority verdict at the top of this document is UNCHANGED, and this is the load-bearing point.** Every item above is read-side: a label, a field, a row, a card, a modal, or a suppressed hypothetical. None of it reaches `assess()`. The §55 staleness rule is the only one that alters behaviour under a live trade, and it alters only the SHADOW verdict — proven by forcing every snapshot stale and observing the entire Tier 0 half of a preview come back byte-identical. `extra_caps` remains unpassed at both production call sites, no registered model is `PRODUCTION`, and §27's stress veto still does not exist. What batch 3 bought is legibility and provenance for the evidence window, not authority.

## 5. Shadow vs production

### What actually vetoes a trade today

Nine gates, all pre-existing or hardened by this programme, in `apps/gateway/routers/orders.py:316-328` and `libs/trading_core/risk/engine.py:436-694`:

| Layer | Control | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Account permission | Trading pool authorization; instrument matrix; 10 permission flags; naked shorts rejected at startup (`config.py:152-158`) | **REJECT / degrade** |
| Data quality | Bar staleness ≤ 5 days; broker cash unreadable → fail closed (`orders.py:293,435-445,1231-1264`) | **FAIL the chain** |
| Tier 0 hard limits | Kill switch; portfolio heat gate; signal tier budget + 1.5% NAV absolute ceiling; single-name risk; single-name capital; static correlation bucket; heat headroom; regime cash floor; portfolio greek limits | **REJECT / RESIZE** |
| Sizing | `budget = min(tier_budget × vol_multiplier, abs_max_trade_risk)` — multiplicative then hard-capped, so no signal tier can undo a vol reduction | **RESIZE** |

Two things to note. The vol multiplier in force is a **crude RV20 proxy** (`portfolio.py:617-635`) that ignores correlation and weights cash at zero vol — not the EWMA or GARCH conditional forecast §14 asks for. And the greek limits REJECT rather than RESIZE, which is an open user question.

### What computes, logs and displays but decides nothing

| Layer | Built | Decision authority |
|---|---|---|
| STATISTICAL | Historical/Gaussian VaR & ES at 95/99, EWMA conditional vol, filtered-HS conditional VaR/ES, portfolio σ, distribution diagnostics, model dispersion | **None** |
| CONCENTRATION | Euler ES contributions, single-name ES share cap (0.35), bucket ES share cap (0.50), incremental ES cap (1.5% NAV), portfolio ES limit (5% NAV) | **None** |
| STRESS | Historical windows, auto-worst-window search, hypothetical grid, user scenarios, full option revaluation, `STRESS_LOSS_LIMIT` at 10% NAV | **None** |
| MODEL HEALTH | Per-model ACTIVE/DEGRADED/UNAVAILABLE/FAILED, model-risk state LOW/ELEVATED/HIGH, GARCH→EWMA fallback | **None — and no cap at all, not even hypothetical** |
| VALIDATION | Walk-forward Kupiec/Christoffersen/LR_cc, ES severity, QLIKE, GREEN/YELLOW/RED verdict, EWMA-vs-GARCH comparison | **None — "promotion is a user action"** |
| LIQUIDITY | Underlying liquidity gate (gate 7) | **REPORT mode — always PASS** |

**The boundary is enforced mechanically, not by discipline.** `engine.py:396,628` defaults `extra_caps=()` so the cap loop is a no-op; both production call sites omit the argument (I verified `orders.py:1748` and `income.py:333` directly); and `tests/test_risk_adversarial.py:1664-1727` is an **AST-level** scan — not a text grep — that fails if any `assess()`/`assess_income()` call in `apps/` is passed `extra_caps`, firing even on an accidental empty tuple. Byte-identity of `assess()` was proven three independent ways, and 40 sabotaged statistical entry points across 12 modules leave 240 Tier 0 decisions byte-identical. The registry is asserted non-PRODUCTION by test, and I confirmed by direct import: `garch11` RESEARCH, four SHADOW, none PRODUCTION.

### The promotion path

Mechanically one line — populating `extra_caps` at `orders.py:1748`. Everything else is judgement, and the report is right that the judgement is not yet available:

1. **Accumulate evidence.** ≥ 20 trading days of logged shadow decisions (audit Q3). **Counter stands at 0** — the programme finished the same day the first SCHEDULED snapshot was written (`final-report.md:11`, 2026-08-18 14:59 UTC).
2. **Fix the evidence pipeline first.** Two Tier A gaps block the window from producing what it is supposed to: §65's seven missing metrics mean there is no time series of how often a shadow gate *would have* bound, and §59's missing shadow budget effect means model risk contributes nothing to the hypothetical quantity being logged. **Close these before counting the 20 days, or the window measures less than it appears to.**
3. **Choose a fail-closed rule.** Today statistical and stress caps fail *open* on an UNAVAILABLE view (`stress.py:955-1004`), and `is_stale` has no consumer (§55). The final report volunteers this itself as G.1 row 7: safe while SHADOW, unsafe the moment it is promoted. Decide the rule for both UNAVAILABLE and stale **before** flipping anything.
4. **Validate the thresholds.** Every number in the shadow layer — 0.35, 0.50, 1.5%, 5%, 10% NAV, the 1.5 dispersion ratio, the 0.80 correlation level — is an explicitly UNVALIDATED research default. They were chosen honestly (`pretrade.py:645-675` quotes the spec back at itself and labels them so) but chosen, not measured.
5. **Promote per model, not per switch.** Each model carries its own `mode`; a single global flip would promote unvalidated models alongside validated ones.
6. ~~**Fix §21 before trusting ES-based caps on option books.**~~ **DONE 2026-08-19.** ES caps are no longer computed on `DELTA_LINEAR` option P&L: both the book and the pre-trade candidate are full-revalued through `bs_price` when the chain supplies an IV, so a cap now sees spot convexity on the positions it exists to control. **The revised caution before promotion:** the estimator is spot-only, so a cap still understates long-VEGA tails, and a SPREAD is priced on its long leg alone — an upper bound on convexity, which is the safe direction for a cap but means the number is not the spread's true tail. Check `data_quality.pnl_method_by_key` before trusting any ES cap on a given book: rows labelled `DELTA_LINEAR` are the ones whose chain gave no IV, and they carry the old limitation unchanged.

### Bottom line

The programme did the hard and unglamorous thing correctly: it left the working risk controls alone, proved it left them alone, built the statistical layer beside them rather than inside them, and labelled everything it could not validate. Its own final report argues against its numbers where warranted. What is missing is not sophistication — the deferrals are well-judged and the models that exist are well-built — but the small unrecorded pieces that make the next step possible: the observability metrics, the model-risk budget effect, and a handful of diagnostics rated "trivial" that were never written down as dropped. Those are cheap, and they should be closed before anyone counts to twenty.
